# US Data Privacy requirement citations

Every USDP checklist row carries its state's act, the section addressing that
practice, and the cited provision's effective date. Programs show the citation
beside the requirement and retain it in the program JSON export. Excluded rows
keep their citations. Citation metadata does not change applicability decisions,
control mappings, observations or an accepted library revision.

The checklist uses Evidence's summaries, not a reproduction of statutory text.
Counsel confirms exemptions, thresholds, amendments, age ranges and phased
compliance against the source law. The date on the state applicability panel is
the act's initial effective date; a requirement's citation can have a later date.
An opt-out date does not claim that Evidence operates the business's website or
that a particular signal was received or honored.

The shipped catalogue adds references to the existing October 2026 checklist;
it is not a claim that every later statutory amendment has been implemented.
Connecticut's amended applicability threshold still follows the installation's
accepted library revision and the owner's state-change review.

Some consolidated rows need a qualification:

- California's cited automated-decisionmaking and assessment provisions delegate
  rulemaking. Their implementing regulations set the actual scope and phased
  compliance deadlines. Cross-context behavioral advertising is sharing under
  the CPRA; it does not mean every form of advertising.
- Utah and Iowa use notice and an opportunity to opt out for adult sensitive
  data. The cited child provisions apply COPPA, rather than a universal rule
  for every teenager. Maryland restricts sensitive-data processing to what is
  strictly necessary and prohibits its sale; consent does not waive that limit.
- Maryland's technology-based authorized-agent route does not itself mandate a
  separate universal opt-out mechanism. Texas and Nebraska also qualify agent
  requests by the conditions in their cited sections.
- Iowa's retained minimization row cites related rights and de-identified-data
  limits, not a general controller minimization duty. Rhode Island's related
  section requires a processor to return or delete data at the end of service.
  Both broader checklist practices remain visible and mapped for counsel review;
  the references do not claim the statutes impose those broader practices.
