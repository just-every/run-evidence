# Evidence target contract

Target date: **16 October 2026**. Evidence is an owned security compliance automation and customer-trust product for a US security or operations lead at a 20–250 person business. A buyer can replace Vanta, Drata or Secureframe for framework readiness, continuous control testing, policy and employee compliance, remediation, risk/vendor management, auditor collaboration and customer security reviews. Comp AI, Sprinto and Delve are the comparison set for the next step: agents carry a failed control to a narrowly authorized fix and fresh source proof, while preserving the original failure.

This is the product to build toward, not a description of today’s release. The owner-approved 16 October target replaces the earlier first-release exclusions of frameworks, policy templates, collectors and questionnaires. Existing immutable receipts, honest gaps, imports and reviewer folders remain the provenance layer within the complete job. One installation serves one business and its scoped teams, employees, customers and auditors. The product stays independently usable and MIT-licensed; shared Base code or sibling integrations do not combine products.

`done` means the entire named clause has implementing code and cited public-door tests at the status baseline. `partial` means only the explicitly stated subset exists or acceptance is incomplete. `missing` means the target behavior has no implementing product path. Future acceptance journeys below are requirements, not claims that planned screens/routes exist. Current `seed.json` metadata and README first-release descriptions have not been changed in this documents-only round and do not narrow this target.

## Published clauses

### Immutable proof and collection

**PROOF-001 — Immutable exhibits and attributable receipts.** An owner creates Control → Requirement → Exhibit → Receipt through `/app`. The server reads stored bytes before issuing a receipt, records server time and authenticated actor, ignores forged digest/origin fields, appends explicit same-requirement replacements, and retains original bytes and facts. Each read verifies size and SHA-256. Source-observed, manually-asserted and imported remain distinct; a fingerprint proves unchanged stored bytes, not truth or operation of a control. The detailed receipt rules below remain binding.

**Acceptance evidence:** Use `/app/controls` and the requirement upload form, attempt forged receipt facts, replace an exhibit, reload its original receipt and download both versions. Attempt a double replacement, oversized upload and changed/missing object: refuse unsafe writes/downloads and retain facts. `tests/evidence.mjs` exercises these production HTTP doors.

**GAP-001 — Honest period-specific proof coverage.** `/app` and `/api/desk` derive covered, due soon, overdue, missing, superseded-only or unavailable bytes per requirement and selected UTC cadence period. Imported history cannot establish current proof. Storage failure is unavailable, never green. Historical completeness describes recorded assertions and remains separate from observed control effectiveness. The detailed period rules below remain binding. Semi-annual, annual and anchored renewal periods are CADENCE-001; they add periods and never change these four.

**Acceptance evidence:** At the desk select daily, weekly, monthly and quarterly periods, advance across their deadlines and inspect a superseded period, imported-only proof and changed/missing R2 bytes. Reopen a completed historical period and confirm the original state logic. `tests/evidence.mjs` exercises these cases through `/api/desk` and receipt downloads.

**COLLECTION-001 — Scheduled source collection with visible failures.** Owner-authorized collectors run without an open browser on configurable schedules and source events. Every run records account/asset coverage, connector and control revision, source and receipt timestamps, audit period, cursor, completeness and a terminal outcome. Source-observed evidence requires a successful authorized read; manual uploads and imported artifacts cannot impersonate it. Pagination, partial permissions, throttling, expired grants, dropped assets and schema drift create explicit gaps. Bounded backoff resumes collection without duplicate receipts or fabricated catch-up observations.

**Acceptance evidence:** Connect a real authorized sandbox via the Connections UI, schedule collection, close the browser, change the source and inspect the next run. Revoke a required scope, throttle a page and interrupt a run; reconnect and resume. The run history and period report preserve missing coverage, failure and recovery separately; only actual source reads issue observed receipts.

**Current partial slice (22 October 2026):** The twelve hosted collectors are GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket Cloud, Azure DevOps, AWS, Google Cloud, Azure, Okta, Google Workspace, Entra ID, JumpCloud and KnowBe4. Whole-credential rejection and owner revocation immediately append unknown test evaluations, and current readiness refuses withdrawn or not-yet-reread renewed authority without rewriting earlier observations. Owners set elapsed schedules of 5–60 minutes in whole multiples of five (default 60), measured in UTC since the last attempt, on the next five-minute Cron tick. Backoff takes precedence. Schedule changes are audited and preserve provider configuration. A missed interval creates no catch-up observation. Private Git keeps its computer's own scheduler and refuses hosted scheduling and source-event grants.

Every attempt retains its interval, account/provider, connector revision, accepted library revision, enabled test criteria/revisions, linked control definitions and installation audit-period revisions. Later definition, mapping, library and program changes never reinterpret earlier snapshots. Successful and partial authorized reads have a source-read start; every terminal attempt has its receipt time, outcome and original gaps. These are collector clocks, not invented provider modification times: an unreported source-event time stays unknown, and a forwarded reported time stays explicitly unverified. Owner-only connection and control period reports retain earlier failures and later recovery separately, plus elapsed windows without an attempt. Historical rows without captured context stay unavailable.

Consumed security fields are validated against the provider's documented response shapes. GitHub checks the requested organization and selected repository identity (case-insensitive canonical names), refuses foreign-owner repository listings, and validates repository/protection/alert fields. Azure checks list envelopes and identities, safe same-resource continuations, storage/SQL/network fields, enabled log destinations and all three Defender plan tiers. Google Cloud checks lists, project identity, IAM/audit configuration, storage/IAM, service accounts/keys, SQL and firewalls. Directory readers check identity, lifecycle, admin/MFA/timestamp/group facts. KnowBe4 admits completions only for exact completed states, valid identities and dates and complete non-duplicated enrollment pages. AWS checks STS/IAM/credential reports, S3, CloudTrail, EBS, security-group CIDRs/ports, RDS and GuardDuty. Class-specific malformed, denied, incomplete or repeated pages remain explicit historical gaps with known affected subjects unknown. A disabled API and explicit empty collections stay distinct from missing facts. Azure SQL refuses more than 50 servers before reading a subset; AWS refuses truncated bucket/account scopes. Current catalog/manual-replacement semantics are unchanged.

Hosted paged reads retain durable continuations before the next network request. GitHub retains alert numbers and same-endpoint cursors (64 KiB); the other readers retain only declared source fields, compressed into the existing custody-preserved run record (8 MiB decoded and within the row's remaining 240,000-character metadata budget). An incomplete read may resume for at most one hour, with its original source-read time. Authority, selected scope/configuration and control/test/library/audit-period context changes require fresh reads. Completed top-level inventories and class lists refresh on retry; completed nested account lookups can be reused only within the same incomplete read. Unsafe/repeated continuations or storage bounds refuse completeness; unusable/expired checkpoints require fresh authorized reads. No cached fact acquires a fabricated fresh timestamp. Original failed/throttled runs and checkpoints survive recovery and inactive restore. The source-provider tests use official-schema network-edge fakes; no live provider validation is claimed.

**Source-event default approved with riders (ct, 22 October 2026 23:45Z):** Forwarded events use `/v1/connections/{id}/events`, an expiring `resource-push` API token and a separate owner-only, per-connection grant with a recorded reason. New connections deny events. Store only the token digest, show the token once and never export it. Expiry or failed registration destroys token authority; restoration leaves it inactive (WORK-001). Native provider webhook provisioning is not claimed. The event is an unverified hint: only its fingerprint, authenticated token reference, received time and optional explicitly unverified reported time enter the durable queue; no payload becomes evidence and an event never issues an evidence receipt. Only an actual authorized source read produces source-observed evidence; API-001 pushed records remain source-reported. The next scheduler run performs a real authorized read even before the elapsed interval is due, honoring source backoff. Duplicate event ids for the same token and connection share one queue entry; replay and duplicate deliveries are idempotent. Restart reconciles an already committed collection before another read; pending events cannot revive restored token or connection authority. Removing a grant, revocation and expiry prevent pending dispatch. Owner pages show original event queue records and collection outcomes. API-001, AUTH-001, SECURE-001 and later source providers under CODE-001, CLOUD-002, APPS-001, MONITOR-001, HRIS-001 and DEVICE-001 inherit these event-authentication, no-payload-evidence, audit and restore obligations. The grant and its reason are audited. COLLECTION-001 remains PARTIAL for the remaining acceptance and named Gaps below.

### Audit folders and questionnaires

**REVIEW-001 — Selected expiring evidence folders.** Owners select controls, an inclusive date range and expiry at `/app/folders`. A one-time bearer link exposes only the scoped read-only projection, checked on every request and again before disclosure. Closing is terminal; unknown, changed, closed, expired and out-of-scope requests receive the same plain 404. Tokens never appear in stored owner lists, events or archives. Originals inside a folder scope remain visible even after redacted replacement; inspect the full list before sharing. The detailed reviewer rules below remain binding.

**Acceptance evidence:** Issue a folder through `/api/folders`, open its `/review/{token}` page and download only in-period exhibits. Try a crossing-period receipt, foreign control, write, altered token, expiry during a read and closed folder. Owner event history retains allowed/refused attempts without raw links. `tests/review.mjs` and `tests/review-browser.mjs` cover these doors.

**ANSWER-001 — Cited security-questionnaire completion.** Upload XLSX, CSV, DOCX and PDF questionnaires (PDF capture, per-question collaboration and answer-library import are ANSWER-003; portal questionnaires are ANSWER-002), preserve question IDs, sections and required answer format, and draft answers only from authorized approved policies, control observations, audit reports and prior approved answers. Every material assertion cites exact source revision and relevant period; unsupported or contradictory questions stay flagged. A named security/legal reviewer approves the exact answer set and recipient before export or submission. Export preserves the buyer’s template; reusable answers expire or invalidate when sources change. Each draft reports counts of cited, uncited-but-flagged and conflicting answers. An answer is either cited to sources that support every assertion in it or flagged; it is never drafted uncited.

Before shipping, this clause adds questionnaire completion records to the READINESS-001 checklist, with reconciled counts and record drilldown.

**Acceptance evidence:** Against a populated program (approved policies, passing and failing tests, a SOC 2 report), upload a full-length SIG Lite (XLSX) and CSA CAIQ v4 (XLSX), plus a CSV and a DOCX customer questionnaire, in Questionnaires, and generate cited drafts. A held-out reviewer key, prepared independently, lists for each question whether the populated sources can answer it and which source supports it. Then check:
- the draft reports cited, flagged and conflicting counts that reconcile to the question total;
- at least 90% of the questions the key marks answerable are drafted with citations;
- 100% of citations resolve to the source revision and period they name, and support the claim per the key;
- zero answers are uncited or assert beyond their sources;
- every question the key marks unanswerable is flagged.

Inspect citations, edit and approve an exact version. Modify a source after approval and attempt reuse/submission: require a new decision. Export and reopen the original formats without losing question placement, IDs or flagged gaps.

**LIBRARY-001 — Shipped control library, test catalog and cadence.** Evidence ships a versioned common control library pre-mapped to every requirement of each core program: the SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria (2017, revised points of focus 2022) for Security plus the optional Availability, Confidentiality, Processing Integrity and Privacy categories; ISO 27001:2022 clauses 4–10 and Annex A; the HIPAA Security Rule and the Breach Notification Rule; and PCI DSS v4.0.1. Each shipped control carries at least one automated test or a named evidence requirement, plus linked policy templates (POLICY-001). Each named cloud, identity, code, HRIS and device connector ships a published test catalog covering every evidence class its clause lists:
- **Cloud:** IAM/MFA, public storage, encryption at rest and in transit, logging/monitoring, backup and network configuration.
- **Hosting, edge and managed data (CLOUD-002):** member access and MFA/SSO, encryption and TLS, audit logging, backup and recovery, network restrictions and public exposure, per platform.
- **Identity:** MFA, privileged roles, inactive and departed users.
- **App accounts (APPS-001):** departed person with an active account, admin without MFA, local login bypassing required SSO, privileged guest and inactive account.
- **Code:** branch protection, required review and checks, membership and MFA, repository privacy, and dependency/secret alerts.
- **Vulnerability (VULN-001):** findings past their SLA due date, and critical/high findings without an owner or exception.
- **Security issues and incidents (ISSUES-001):** security issues resolved within SLA, open security issues past their due date, and incident tasks completed per the incident response policy.
- **Monitoring and alerting (MONITOR-001):** each in-scope production service has availability and error-rate or latency monitors, a security-event alert routes to a person, every monitor routes to a live notification target, no production monitor is muted beyond the owner-set window, and on-call escalation covers every hour of the coming 14 days.
- **HRIS:** roster and lifecycle completeness.
- **Device:** encryption, screen lock, OS/patch, MDM enrollment and EDR sensor health.

A platform that does not expose an evidence class lists it in its catalog as unsupported, together with the manual evidence requirement that replaces it; an unsupported class never counts as passing.

Every connector that exists today (AWS, Google Cloud and Azure under CLOUD-001; Okta, Google Workspace, Entra ID and JumpCloud under IDENTITY-001; GitHub under CODE-001 and CONNECT-001) ships its catalog and is enumerated by the build-time check. Each catalog entry states the criteria, evaluated resource type, mapped controls and cadence, plus remediation steps for every provider it runs against: the console steps, the equivalent CLI or API call and, for cloud and hosting configuration tests on a platform with a Terraform provider, a Terraform snippet that sets the passing configuration. A platform without a Terraform provider has its entry say so. A test's Results page shows these steps in a How to remediate panel for the provider of each failing resource, and the guidance on its REMEDY-001 obligation is drawn from them, pinned to the library revision. Scheduled configuration tests run at least once per hour per connected account, and population and period tests at least daily. Each catalog entry and the readiness view show the last-run time, and a missed run appears as a stale interval, never as a carried-forward pass. Library updates arrive as reviewable revisions and never retroactively change results.

Library revisions work as follows. Every installation starts at shipped library revision 1. Each later shipped revision is a change set of control-to-requirement mappings, listed under Control library (`/app/library`, `/api/library`) with the programs on this installation it would affect. **API-001 — Public REST API for pushed resources, custom tests and program state.** A versioned, documented public REST API (OpenAPI 3.1 specification published with each release) uses scoped, expiring, revocable API tokens bound to one installation and named scopes. **REMEDY-002 — Remediation SLAs and automatic re-test.** Owners define SLA policies by test category (cloud, identity, code, device, vulnerability, security issue, personnel, policy, vendor) and severity, with a default policy shipped per category. Each failing user, device, account, resource or vulnerability finding gets its own SLA clock starting at its first failing observation. A vulnerability finding's due date is fixed at first detection from the SLA policy revision then in force (VULN-001); changing a policy applies only to findings first detected afterwards. A tracker-sourced security issue (ISSUES-001) is clocked the same way from its tracker creation time, and its closure is recorded as the tracker's resolution rather than a verified pass. The clock pauses only for an approved, expiring exception and resets only on verified pass. Readiness, test history, the remediation queue and board/auditor exports show each item as within SLA, approaching or SLA missed, with time-to-remediate history filterable by period, owner and category. SLA breaches escalate through FOLLOWUP-001. A linked task closing in Evidence or in any WORK-001 tracker, or a source change event, automatically queues a re-test within 15 minutes, with no human action; a per-item ticket (WORK-002) re-tests only its item. If that re-test fails, the obligation reopens and the SLA clock keeps running.

SLA days are 24-hour UTC intervals. Approved exception intervals are clipped to the running failure cycle and counted as a union, so overlap never pauses time twice; expiry or revocation ends its interval. The running cycle keeps its policy revision and original first-failure time until a verified pass; a regression after that pass starts a new cycle under the policy then in force. ## Retained provenance mechanics

These rules define existing receipt, cadence, bearer-folder and custody behavior. Target program automation adds independently attributable observations and permissions; it must not relabel manual/imported material, silently change historical period logic or grant external authority on restore. Explicit erasure under ERASE-001 is the only target exception to ordinary immutable-record deletion rules.

## Receipt facts

The receipt origin vocabulary is exactly source-observed, manually-asserted, or imported. The manual upload route always writes manually-asserted; a caller cannot select imported or source-observed. Caller-supplied origin, actor, digest and time are ignored. The authenticated Base owner identity and server UTC timestamp are recorded. Source and inclusive covered-from/through UTC dates are owner assertions, including future coverage; the desk does not verify their truth.

The server writes a fresh random R2 key, then reads the stored bytes and computes SHA-256. A filename is display metadata, never a key or filesystem path. Each upload appends an Exhibit and Receipt in a D1 transaction. Supersession requires an explicit current receipt belonging to the same requirement. The newer receipt points to the old; no old fact or byte is changed. A unique constraint prevents concurrent double replacements. Old files remain listed and downloadable. Receipt/exhibit update and deletion are forbidden by SQLite triggers. An interrupted storage/DB operation may leave unreferenced bytes, but never a successful invented receipt.

Files are nonempty and limited to 10 MiB; the multipart body is streamed with a 10 MiB + 64 KiB cap before parsing. Downloads require owner authorization, force attachment and octet-stream, include nosniff and a sandbox policy, and do not preview HTML/SVG. Every read verifies actual R2 bytes against retained size and SHA-256. Missing, unreadable or changed bytes are unavailable; the receipt remains.

## Period and gap rules

Dates are real ISO calendar dates in UTC. On the owner desk, the selected date chooses a whole calendar period: daily midnight to midnight; weekly Monday through Sunday; monthly first through last day; quarterly Jan–Mar, Apr–Jun, Jul–Sep, Oct–Dec. Covered-through is inclusive; the deadline is the next UTC midnight. The default is today's UTC date; the owner can select a historical date. These four calendar periods are the current desk periods; the semi-annual, annual and anchored periods of CADENCE-001 extend them without changing any rule in this section.

A matching receipt must cover the entire selected period and cannot have imported origin. Imported material is supplied history; a fresh owner assertion is needed to establish current proof. A receipt is current only when no newer receipt supersedes it, regardless of its covered period. Precedence is:

1. Any matching current receipt whose actual bytes cannot be read and verified: unavailable bytes, even if another matching receipt is available.
2. Matching current receipts with verified bytes: due soon in the final 20% of that selected period before its deadline; otherwise covered. For daily proof, the final 4 hours 48 minutes are due soon. Historical complete periods stay covered. This is completeness of recorded assertions, not control effectiveness.
3. Matching receipts exist but all have been superseded: superseded-only. A normal replacement may assert a different covered period; selecting the older period then reaches this state without invented records.
4. No receipts at all: missing.
5. No matching receipts, and the selected period has ended: overdue. On today's default view it is also overdue when any current receipt's covered-through has expired, making covered → due soon → overdue reachable across the calendar boundary. Otherwise: missing.

R2 or D1 failures that prevent a coherent desk return an explicit unavailable response. No failed read asserts coverage. Time is server time; only the test harness can inject a clock function. There is no production time header, query or environment override.

## Current reviewer-folder mechanics

An owner issues a folder with a reviewer label, one or more existing controls, a start and end date, and a future UTC expiry. The dates are inclusive and the start cannot follow the end. Duplicate control selections collapse to one selection. Issuing creates a random 256-bit bearer token. The creation response is its only display; persistent storage keeps its SHA-256 digest, not the raw token. Owner folder listings and revisits cannot recover the link. Treat the link as a secret: anyone who has it may use the selected reviewer doors until the folder closes or expires.

The reviewer does not sign in and receives no owner session or mutation route. Each page, list, receipt and download request re-reads the stored grant. Authorization requires the matching token, an open folder, a request before expiry, the expected local origin, a read-only GET/HEAD method and the current control and period selection. Expiry is exact: at now equal to expires_at the request is refused. The grant is rechecked immediately before a response is disclosed, so a close or expiry during a storage read cannot preserve a stale response. Public pages and responses are no-store and use no-referrer.

The folder's date interval is inclusive. A receipt is visible only when its whole covered-from/through interval is inside that interval and its requirement belongs to a selected control. A receipt that crosses either boundary is excluded from the list and from direct receipt/view/download access. The gap for each selected requirement uses the r1 calculation at the folder's end date. Covered, due soon and unavailable bytes use only in-scope receipts. Full history preserves whether an in-scope receipt was superseded, and the full-history overdue result is used only when the scoped calculation would be missing. A gap can therefore show overdue or superseded-only because of hidden history, but hidden rows cannot establish covered or unavailable bytes. The hidden receipt fields and bytes stay private.

Reviewer projections contain the selected control and requirement, cadence, gap, in-scope receipt facts, origin and source, period, fingerprint, filename, size, media type, availability, and the in-scope supersession relationship. They omit uploader or owner identity, object-storage keys, private owner routes, other controls/folders and review logs. Downloads are verified attachment/octet-stream responses with nosniff and sandbox policy; unavailable bytes do not produce a successful download. Unknown, changed, closed, expired and out-of-scope requests use the same plain 404 response on reviewer doors. Storage failure returns an unavailable response rather than asserting access or coverage.

Owner-only event history records allowed and refused page/list/receipt/download attempts with time and known folder/exhibit association. Unknown-token attempts have no invented folder association. Events contain no raw token or token-bearing URL. The current bearer-folder projection cannot see event logs or write findings. Engagement findings and auditor decisions belong to AUDIT-001; they do not grant bearer-folder mutation authority. Closing is terminal; reissue means creating a new folder. Closing cannot revoke a copy a reviewer already downloaded.

Exhibits remain private locally and immutable. There is no automatic pruning or per-receipt allowlist on a review folder: every receipt whose full period is inside the selected interval for a selected control is in scope, including superseded history. Inspect the whole projected list before sharing. A redacted replacement is a new receipt and, when replacing existing proof, explicitly supersedes the prior receipt; the original receipt and bytes stay in history. A redacted replacement does not hide an original that is also inside the folder's control/date scope. A complete archive includes both. Closing a folder cannot revoke a copy a reviewer already downloaded.

## Current custody-archive mechanics

The owner archive contains the Evidence controls, requirements, exhibits, receipt facts, receipt-actor provenance, review folders and selected controls, and review events. Archive version 2 also retains import reviews, native links, Base import custody tables and original ZIPs, plus agent token metadata, snapshots and decisions. Version 1 archives remain readable. It never contains reviewer or agent token hashes, owner sessions, passwords or authentication credentials. Restored agent credentials and source-owner import authority remain inactive. Receipt actor IDs and display names are preserved as historical provenance without creating active destination accounts. Restored folders are closed and have no grant hash; their event history remains. Issue a new equivalent folder if review access is needed again.

The manifest records each exhibit's archive path, expected size and SHA-256, plus whether verified bytes were available when inventoried. A complete archive streams all available verified exhibit bytes. Missing, unreadable or changed bytes remain listed with a reason and keep their receipt facts; unavailable bytes are not silently represented as present. The manifest has a digest over its exact serialized bytes, and each file is checked against its retained receipt digest. If an object changes after inventory and before export, export aborts rather than returning an inconsistent archive.

Restore accepts only an empty Evidence domain and exhibit bucket; destination authentication may already exist. It validates the manifest digest, format, relationships and all entries before writing business rows or exhibit objects. It refuses unsafe paths (absolute, traversal, drive or backslash paths), duplicate or unlisted entries, absent entries marked available, incorrect lengths or digests, unsupported versions, excessive metadata, and nonempty targets. A refusal on an empty destination leaves its business records and bucket empty; a nonempty-target refusal leaves that target's existing data untouched. Restore verifies stored bytes before admitting rows, then adds the records in a database transaction. Receipt facts, origin, actors and supersession are preserved. An unavailable entry remains unavailable after restore.

The archive CLI is separate from Base's generic export/import. It provides a streamed export and a dry-run or confirmed restore. Stop dev before using the local CLI. Its standard output is summary JSON; export dry-run adds per-file inventory descriptors with exhibit IDs, paths, sizes, digests and availability, but no business table rows or internal storage keys. Keep that inventory private metadata. The archive file contains the full manifest, including internal object keys, and available original exhibits; keep it private and do not overwrite the original archive.

Legacy v1–v9 archives retain their bounded metadata validators: at most 8 MiB and 20,000 rows, with exhibit files at most 10 MiB. New v10 archives page records in groups of at most 128 rows (a 512 KiB soft page target; individual frames refuse above 16 MiB), stream original bytes in 256 KiB parts, and upload in 1 MiB transport chunks. Restores stage original files with multipart storage and validate every frame and final digest before transactional admission. The former 20,000-row and 64 MiB aggregate boundaries do not apply to v10; original files still obey their public upload doors’ per-file limits. Explicit staging is private, resumable and separate from admitted business data. An unrecognized table, column or object namespace refuses export rather than silently omitting data.
